01 — Overview

What is this project?

Webex Calling + AI Receptionist

AI Receptionist is a voice AI agent for Webex Calling. It answers incoming calls in natural language, works out what the caller wants, and either helps them directly or transfers them to the right person or team.

Admins configure it in Control Hub. Every decision they make there, from the greeting to what happens when a call runs long, shapes a real conversation with a real customer.

My Role

I led UX for the AI Receptionist admin portal end to end, across seven parts of the setup journey. Each one answers a question an admin has to settle before they’ll let an AI talk to their customers.

The Problem in One Sentence

Admins were being asked to hand their front line to an AI, using tools built for menus and keypresses.

TL;DR

Before

  • Auto attendant menus: “Press 1 for sales”
  • Callers adapt to the menu tree
  • Transfers by extension number
  • No view of what callers actually asked
  • Calls end when the menu runs out

After

  • Callers say what they need, in their own words
  • Admins define intents and what each one does
  • Transfers by spoken name, with clarification
  • Recordings and transcripts for every session
  • Clear limits and fallbacks, set by the admin

03 — Start

Draft — check copy against shipped product

From blank page to first call.

Guided setup

The problem it solved

An empty AI configuration gives admins nothing to react to. They didn’t know where to begin, or whether the receptionist would sound right before customers heard it.

The design decisionA short guided start that asks only what’s needed to go live, fills in sensible defaults, and ends with a test call before anything reaches customers.

  • Name, greeting, and voice set up front
  • Business hours and language chosen in the same flow
  • Assign a phone number when the admin is ready
  • Test call before going live

Trade-off → Fewer choices up front meant moving advanced settings out of setup. Admins reach them later, once the basics work.

Process 01 — Intents

Draft — replace sample intents with real examples

Teaching it what callers want.

Intent configuration

The problem it solved

Admins think in outcomes (“send billing questions to accounts”), not in models and prompts. The setup had to speak their language.

The design decisionEach intent is a plain-language pairing: what the caller might want, a few example phrases, and what the receptionist should do about it.

IntentCaller might sayThen
Billing question“I have a question about my invoice.”Transfer to Accounts
Opening hours“Are you open on Saturday?”Answer directly
Book an appointment“Can I come in next week?”Use integration
Speak to someone“Can I talk to Maria?”Transfer by name
Anything elseNot matched to an intentFallback transfer

Trade-off → A fallback is always required. Admins can’t save an intent list that leaves a caller with nowhere to go.

Process 02 — Transfer by name

Draft — confirm directory rules

“Can I speak to Alex?”

Directory transfers

The problem it solved

Callers know names, not extensions. But names repeat, sound alike, and some people shouldn’t be reachable from the front line at all.

The design decisionLet admins choose who can be reached by name, and have the receptionist ask a short clarifying question whenever a name matches more than one person.

What admins control

  • Which users and groups are reachable
  • People excluded from transfers
  • How to tell matching names apart
  • Where the call goes if no one answers

In the conversation

CallerCan I speak to Alex Chen?

ReceptionistI have two people named Alex Chen. Is that Alex in Sales, or Alex in Finance?

CallerSales, please.

Transferring to Alex Chen · Sales

The philosophy

A human receptionist would ask. So should the AI. One short question beats a confident wrong transfer.

What if the name isn’t found?

The receptionist says so plainly and offers the fallback, instead of guessing.

What about executives?

Admins can keep anyone off the list, so private lines stay private.

Process 03 — Recording

Draft — confirm compliance wording

Recording, with consent built in.

Call recording

Recording settingsScreenshot · 16:9
The problem it solved

Admins need recordings to check the AI’s work, but recording rules vary by region and by company policy. A single toggle hid too much.

The design decisionPair the recording setting with the announcement callers hear, so turning one on means deciding the other.

  • Recording on or off per receptionist
  • Announcement played at the start of the call
  • Recordings linked from each session

Trade-off → An extra step for admins in exchange for fewer compliance surprises later.

Process 04 — Call transcripts

Draft — replace sample transcript

Seeing what the AI heard.

Session transcripts

The problem it solved

When a call went wrong, admins couldn’t tell why. Was it the caller, the intent, or the setup? Without evidence, trust in the AI stalled.

The design decisionShow each session as a readable conversation, with the receptionist’s decisions marked inline, so admins can trace a call from greeting to outcome.

  • Turn-by-turn transcript with timestamps
  • Matched intent and action shown in place
  • Outcome at the top: transferred, answered, or ended

Why it matters → Transcripts turn “the AI got it wrong” into a specific intent an admin can fix.

Process 05 — Integrations

Draft — replace with actual integration types

Doing more than routing.

Connected systems

The problem it solved

Many calls are simple tasks, like booking a time or checking a status, that still ended in a transfer because the receptionist couldn’t reach the business’s systems.

The design decisionMake integrations something an intent can use, so admins connect a system once and then choose which caller requests it handles.

Scheduling

Book appointments

Check availability and book a time while the caller is still on the line.

Knowledge

Answer common questions

Use the business’s own information for hours, locations, and policies.

Records

Look up details

Pull an order or account status so the caller doesn’t need a person.

Process 06 — Session time limit

Draft — confirm limits and defaults

Ending a call well.

Session time limit

The problem it solved

An AI conversation can loop, or a caller can stay on indefinitely. Without a limit, costs and lines tie up. With a hard cutoff, callers get dropped mid-sentence.

The design decisionLet admins set a maximum session length, warn the caller before it’s reached, and always hand off to a fallback instead of hanging up.

ConversationThe receptionist handles the call as usual.
WarningThe caller hears that time is nearly up.
HandoffTransfer to the fallback.

Trade-off → A limit protects the business, the warning and handoff protect the caller. Neither works without the other.

Design Principles

How I thought about putting AI on the front line.

01

Speak the admin’s language

Intents, actions, and fallbacks are described as business outcomes, not model settings.

02

Every path has an exit

Unmatched intents, unknown names, and long sessions all end in a fallback a person controls.

03

Ask instead of guess

When the receptionist isn’t sure, it asks one short question, the way a human receptionist would.

04

Show the evidence

Recordings and transcripts let admins see exactly what happened, so trust is earned call by call.

06 — Reflection

Draft — replace with your own reflections

What I’d do differently.

  1. 01

    Design transcripts first

    Admins’ trust came from seeing real calls. Starting there would have shaped every other setting sooner.

  2. 02

    Test with real call recordings

    Messy, real phrasing would have stress-tested intents and name matching earlier than scripted examples did.

  3. 03

    Plan for Agent Studio from day one

    Knowing the receptionist would join AI Agent Studio would have aligned patterns across both earlier.

Key learnings.

Learning 01

Fallbacks are the product

Admins judged the AI by what happened when it didn’t know. Clear exits earned more trust than clever answers.

Learning 02

Design the conversation, not just the settings

Every toggle in the portal becomes something a caller hears. Writing those moments out kept the settings honest.

Learning 03

Visibility builds adoption

Recordings and transcripts turned the AI from a black box into something admins could improve.
